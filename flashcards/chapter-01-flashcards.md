## Chapter 1: Foundations: Security, Compliance, and Identity Concepts — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Shared Responsibility Model: In IaaS, who manages the operating system? | The customer. In IaaS the provider only manages physical infrastructure, host, network, and virtualization — everything above the hypervisor (OS, runtime, applications, data) is the customer's job. Exam tip: responsibility for OS/app patching shifts to the provider as you move IaaS → PaaS → SaaS. |
| 2 | Shared Responsibility Model: What does the CUSTOMER always own, regardless of IaaS/PaaS/SaaS? | Data, identities/accounts, endpoints, and access management. Exam tip: 'information and data' and 'accounts and identities' rows never shift to the provider in any model. |
| 3 | Zero Trust — name the 3 guiding principles | 1) Verify explicitly 2) Use least privilege access 3) Assume breach. Exam tip: memory trick — 'Verify, Limit, Assume' (VLA). |
| 4 | Zero Trust: 'Verify explicitly' means what in practice? | Always authenticate and authorize based on ALL available data points (identity, location, device health, service, data classification) — never trust based on network location alone. |
| 5 | Zero Trust: 'Least privilege access' means what? | Limit user access with Just-In-Time (JIT) and Just-Enough-Access (JEA), risk-based adaptive policies, and data protection to minimize exposure if credentials are compromised. |
| 6 | Defense in Depth — list the typical layers (outer to inner) | Physical security → Identity & access → Perimeter (DDoS, firewalls) → Network → Compute → Application → Data. Exam tip: data sits at the CENTER; identity is called the 'primary perimeter' in modern Zero Trust models. |
| 7 | Encryption vs. Hashing — key difference? | Encryption is reversible (decrypt with a key) and protects confidentiality. Hashing is one-way and used for integrity verification and password storage. Exam tip: hashed data can never be 'decrypted' back to original. |
| 8 | Authentication vs. Authorization — one-line difference | Authentication = proving WHO you are (login/credentials). Authorization = determining WHAT you're allowed to do (permissions/access). Authentication always happens first. |
| 9 | Why is 'Identity' called the primary security perimeter? | With cloud and remote work, the traditional network perimeter is dissolved; identity (user/device verification) becomes the new control boundary for granting access to resources — a core Zero Trust concept. |
| 10 | Phishing vs. Ransomware — how to tell them apart on the exam | Phishing = social engineering to STEAL credentials/info (usually via deceptive email/link). Ransomware = malware that ENCRYPTS data and demands payment. Exam tip: phishing is often the delivery method that leads to a ransomware infection. |
| 11 | DDoS attack — what does it target and what's the goal? | Targets availability by flooding a service with traffic from multiple sources, making it unavailable to legitimate users. Exam tip: DDoS does NOT steal data — its goal is disruption, not theft. |
| 12 | GRC — what do the three letters stand for and what's the exam-tested purpose? | Governance, Risk, Compliance. Governance = policies/oversight; Risk = identifying/mitigating threats to objectives; Compliance = meeting legal/regulatory requirements. Exam tip: GRC is a business framework, NOT a specific tool or product. |
